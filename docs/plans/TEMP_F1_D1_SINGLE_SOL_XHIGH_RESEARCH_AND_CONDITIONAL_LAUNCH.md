## 6. F1 formulation hardening

The session must not treat “F1” as one indivisible claim. Reconstruct and adjudicate at least the following layers.

### F1-A — separability

Actual outcome, producer claim, evidence validity, evidence binding, authority, verification result, acceptance/commitment, and derived research predicate can diverge under a frozen declared boundary.

Current expectation: **direct bounded support exists**, but exact scope must be rechecked.

### F1-B — incremental decision value

Evidence binding and/or authority correctness provide decision-relevant information beyond producer assertion and raw outcome correctness.

Current expectation: **bounded support; generality not established**.

### F1-C — cross-construction generalization

The incremental value survives independently authored tasks, independently grounded evaluators, and nontrivial disagreement rather than only synthetic corruptions whose oracle and perturbation logic share assumptions.

Current expectation: **Not established**.

### F1-D — agency relevance

The F1 relation changes consequential agent decisions — accept, reject, abstain, act, or revise — in a way that cannot be reduced to better experimental bookkeeping or offline evaluation hygiene.

Current expectation: **Not established; central discriminator for foundational status**.

### F1-E — practical non-dominance

Any reduction in false commitment is not systematically dominated by false rejection, abstention burden, verification latency/resources, or other declared decision costs.

Current expectation: **Not established**.

For every layer, record:

- exact formulation;
- unit of analysis;
- horizon;
- evidence class and primary paths;
- strongest rival explanation;
- discriminating prediction;
- falsifier or narrowing condition;
- current status: `supported_within_scope`, `inferred`, `not_established`, `conflicting`, `narrowed`, or `rejected`.

Do not allow evidence for F1-A to silently count as evidence for F1-C, F1-D, or F1-E.

## 7. Mandatory rival theories

D1 is defective unless at least these rivals are formulated strongly enough to beat F1 under some possible outcome.

### R1 — outcome-only / calibrated outcome account

Reliable commitment is adequately determined by sufficiently calibrated task-outcome estimation; provenance/authority structure adds no material decision information once outcome uncertainty is known.

### R2 — semantic-verification-only account

Authority-independent semantic verification captures the useful signal; explicit evidence binding and authority mapping add bookkeeping but no incremental decision value.

### R3 — measurement-substrate account

F1 distinctions are necessary for trustworthy research/evaluation records but are not themselves a theory of reliable agency. The actual agent-science explanandum lies elsewhere, such as outcome optimization, configuration adequacy, or adaptation.

### R4 — full F1 account

Outcome, claim, verification, evidence binding, and authority relations each have conditions under which they contribute independent information to warranted commitment and consequential action.

The session may add stronger rivals, but may not weaken these.

For every rival, state what observation would make it preferable to F1.

- declared task/configuration/verifier/action/horizon boundary.

Required anti-circularity checks:

1. `authority` cannot mean “the source that turned out to be correct”;
2. `independence` cannot be asserted merely because components have different names or processes;
3. the oracle/ground truth cannot be generated by the same transformation that defines an evidence corruption;
4. the outcome label cannot be inferred from the adjudication rule being tested;
5. verifier correctness must remain an empirical property or explicit assumption, not a definition;
6. evidence volume must not substitute for relevance, binding, or authority.

If these constructs cannot be operationalized well enough to distinguish R1–R4, the correct verdict is `scientifically_blocked`; do not launch a model run.

## 9. D1 development objective

Develop the synthesis's “held-out warrant generalization” proposal into a study that can genuinely discriminate F1 from its rivals.

D1 must be split conceptually into two layers.

### 9.1 D1a — model-free instrument and theory discrimination qualification

Purpose:

> establish that the study machinery, task construction, authority/evidence manipulations, ground truth, and adjudication policies can distinguish rival predictions without any new model/provider call.

D1a must be deterministic where possible and must be completed before any D1b launch decision.

At minimum, D1a must specify and qualify:

- independently authored bounded task cases or frozen candidate outputs;
- ground-truth construction independent of the evidence/authority perturbation logic;
- at least one condition where producer claims and outcomes agree;
- at least one where they diverge;
- valid, invalid, stale/crossed/unrelated, or otherwise non-binding evidence where scientifically justified;
- authority-map perturbation that does not alter the underlying task outcome;
- evaluator-disagreement or verifier-failure conditions sufficient to test whether “more verification” trivially solves the problem;
- comparator adjudication policies corresponding to R1–R4;
- negative controls where F1 should add no benefit;
- reproducible outcome/adjudication calculation independent of live model behavior.

### 9.2 D1b — first model-produced warrant-generalization batch

Purpose:

> if D1a shows that the design is non-tautological and discriminating, obtain a first bounded set of model-produced claims/outputs under a frozen producer configuration for later independent collection and adjudication.

D1b is a **first model-produced batch**, not proof of cross-task generalization or final validation of F1.

The XHigh session may prepare D1b fully but may launch it only after all Section 13 admission gates pass.

## 10. D1 comparator structure

The exact smallest design is for the XHigh session to derive, but it must preserve separately observable policies equivalent to:

1. **Outcome/calibration baseline** — makes the best commitment decision available without F1-specific evidence/authority structure.
2. **Semantic-verification-only** — uses an independently grounded semantic check but ignores explicit evidence-binding and/or authority-map information where the design permits.
3. **Evidence-bound** — adds claim-relevant evidence validity and binding.
4. **Authority-aware F1** — additionally requires the frozen authority relation appropriate to the claim/decision.

Do not define one policy as correct by construction. Each must have at least one plausible region in which it could equal or outperform the others.

If a cleaner rival-policy mapping is discovered, document why it is more discriminating before replacing this structure.

## 12. Anti-tautology and leakage audit

Before any live launch, D1 must answer all of these satisfactorily:

- Can R1, R2, or R3 win under a plausible result?
- Are invalid evidence and wrong authority conditions defined independently of the policy being evaluated?
- Are task authorship and oracle construction sufficiently independent from the producer under test?
- Is verifier independence concrete and inspectable rather than nominal?
- Is at least one negative-control condition expected to show no F1 advantage?
- Are analysis rules frozen before live outputs are collected?
- Are model outputs prevented from changing the test definition after observation?
- Are missing/ambiguous observations represented as unknown/abstention rather than forced labels?
- Can a perfect semantic verifier condition reveal that authority/binding adds no incremental value if that is in fact true?
- Can excessive false rejection or cost narrow F1 even if false acceptance falls?

Failure on any material item blocks launch.

## 13. Conditional D1b model-launch admission gate

The XHigh session must issue one explicit verdict.

### `ready_for_launch`

All of the following must be true:

1. F1-A through F1-E are separately formulated and statused.
2. R1–R4 have distinct predictions and at least one plausible winning result each.
3. Constructs pass the anti-circularity audit.

5. D1 design has at least one meaningful negative control.

8. The live call ceiling is finite and **no greater than 6 total provider/model calls**.
9. Retries, routes, fallbacks, escalations, model judges, online prompt optimization, workflow search, and recovery are all disabled unless a construct definition makes one unavoidable; if unavoidable, do not launch under this plan.
10. The experiment can run in a read-only or otherwise explicitly bounded action environment appropriate to the task.
11. Definition, inputs, scripts, and expected artifact schema are committed.
12. Normal repository checks pass with no new unexplained failures.
13. The working tree is clean after the freeze commit.
14. The launch/collection split can be obeyed exactly.

### `needs_revision`

Use this when F1/D1 is promising but one or more resolvable design, implementation, provenance, or admission gaps remain.

Action: write the gap and the smallest corrective step; **do not launch**.

### `scientifically_blocked`

Use this when the proposed constructs or study cannot presently discriminate F1 from its strongest rivals without circularity, leakage, or unsupported assumptions.

Action: explain the blocker and what evidence/conceptual resolution would be required; **do not launch**.

No other verdict may authorize a model call.

## 15. Mandatory two-turn launch / independent collection protocol

The historical IRIS-X two-turn execution governance is binding.

### TURN A — this Sol XHigh session

1. complete F1 hardening and D1a;
2. complete D1b specification and implementation if admitted;
3. freeze and commit definition, inputs, scripts, schemas, and analysis rules;
4. run deterministic preflight/admission checks;
5. verify clean tree and record freeze SHA;
6. launch the D1b model process in the background;
7. redirect stdout/stderr outside the repository;
8. record factual launch metadata: command identity, PID if available, launch timestamp, freeze SHA, external log paths, and intended run artifact path;
9. perform **exactly one immediate launch confirmation** solely to establish that process creation was attempted and whether it had already exited at that one observation;
10. **STOP**.

After launch, TURN A must not:

- poll process state;
- wait for completion;
- sleep and recheck;
- tail logs;
- inspect stdout/stderr content;
- inspect partial or final model output;
- inspect generated run artifacts;
- infer whether the model succeeded;
- adjudicate D1b;
- change F1 status based on uncollected output;
- run a second model task.

Even if the process has already exited at the single immediate check, do not inspect the output.

### TURN B — later independent Sol High validation session

Only after explicit owner follow-up, a **new GPT-5.6 Sol / High session** should:

1. verify freeze SHA and launch metadata;
2. collect process completion and raw artifacts;
3. preserve raw output before interpretation;
4. run deterministic validation/adjudication against the frozen D1 specification;
5. audit provenance, call counts, retries, and unexpected drift;
6. classify the result without retroactively changing D1 criteria;
7. report whether evidence supports retaining, narrowing, splitting, or rejecting any F1 layer;
8. avoid automatically proceeding to a second live batch or intervention experiment.

TURN B is intentionally context-independent from the XHigh reasoning session to reduce self-confirmation and post hoc criterion drift.

## 18. Novelty audit boundary

The XHigh session should perform a targeted novelty audit sufficient to avoid obvious reinvention. It is not expected to prove global novelty.

Compare F1 against materially adjacent work in areas such as:

- agent verification and evaluator reliability;
- selective prediction, abstention, confidence/calibration;
- provenance, lineage, evidence binding, and attestation;
- runtime assurance and action authorization;
- agent reliability and failure detection;
- claim/evidence/authority or argumentation systems where directly relevant.

For each materially adjacent lineage, ask:

1. What is its explanandum?
2. What is its unit of analysis?
3. Does it distinguish outcome, claim, evidence binding, authority, and commitment?
4. Does it experimentally vary those relations while holding outcome fixed?
5. Does it connect them to consequential agent commitment/action rather than only offline evaluation?
6. What would remain original for IRIS-X if that lineage is taken at full strength?

The allowed conclusion includes `novelty_not_established`.
