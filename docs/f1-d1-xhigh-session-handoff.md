## Scientific result

The strongest present conclusion is:

> Claim, outcome, evidence, binding, authority, verification, and decision fields are demonstrably separable in bounded IRIS-X fixtures. It is not yet established that their conjunction is an independently discriminating theory of reliable agentic commitment rather than semantic verification plus standard authorization and research-quality recordkeeping.

The decisive adverse findings are:

1. H-V1a/H-V1b verifier separation is real at the interface but shares an authored oracle with outcome adjudication.
2. H-V1b's favorable evidence-bound label partly encodes evidence sufficiency into the criterion being optimized.
3. No ex ante authority policy from an independent legitimate policy owner is coupled to a separate consequential D1 outcome.
4. Full F1 is observationally confounded with R2 or R5 unless semantic-verifier scope and authorization are fixed independently.
5. No valid calibrated outcome-only comparator exists for the exact future producer configuration.
6. No independently authored held-out task/oracle and verifier package exists.

These are resolvable study-design and provenance gaps. That supports `needs_revision` rather than `scientifically_blocked`.

## Novelty boundary

The bounded literature and preserved-source audit found mature antecedents for every individual F1 component: calibration/selective prediction; provenance and lineage; evidence/claim assurance; attestation; access-control and proof-carrying authorization; runtime assurance; and agent evaluator/safety testing. Accordingly, the session records **`novelty_not_established`**.

A plausible but unproven IRIS-X contribution remains: a factorial, held-out agent study that varies claim–evidence–authority relations while outcome is fixed and evaluates consequential commitment plus full rejection/abstention/resource costs. This is a candidate empirical differentiation, not a novelty claim.
