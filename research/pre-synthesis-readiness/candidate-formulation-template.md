# Candidate Formulation Template

Each candidate must state its scope and primary counterevidence explicitly.

## Explanandum

What phenomenon requires explanation? State what would remain unexplained if
this formulation were absent.

## Unit of Analysis and Horizon

Specify model, agent, configuration, execution, task instance, task
distribution, production/maintenance process, or another unit. Specify the
time/repetition/change horizon and justify any aggregation.

## Core Constructs

List construct meanings for this candidate. Cite source-specific definitions
and identify every deliberate departure or unresolved collision. Do not assume
the readiness construct map supplies canonical definitions.

## Causal or Mechanistic Structure

Describe the proposed relationships, intervention points, mediators,
confounders, and observable consequences. Separate model-intrinsic causes from
system/configuration effects.

## Scientific Nontriviality

Explain why the problem is not reducible to implementation convenience,
framework assembly, benchmark optimization, or a feature roadmap.

## Relationship to Success, Reliability, and Cost

State whether these are explananda, outcomes, constraints, decision criteria,
or boundary variables. Define the cost boundary and do not assume a unified
reliability scalar.

## Relationship to Phase 1-6 Evidence

Identify direct support, indirect support, neutral evidence, and challenges.
Preserve task/configuration/horizon bounds and historical result classes.

## Primary Source Support

For each formulation-defining claim, cite the original repository authority,
state the evidence class, and explain source dependence. Do not count a paper,
its implementation, and an IRIS-X synthesis as three independent supports.

## Conflicting and Negative Evidence

Include invalid attempts, false accepts, unverified successes, systemic
failures, authority defects, null/negative results, and absent comparisons.

## Explanatory Compression

What heterogeneous observations become jointly intelligible? What distinctions
would this candidate erase or distort?

## Discriminating Predictions

List predictions that differ from the strongest alternatives. Each prediction
must identify a unit, intervention, observable, horizon, and expected
direction/pattern without inventing an unjustified threshold.

## Falsifiers

State observations that would reject, split, or materially revise this
candidate. Include a result that would favor the strongest alternative.

## Operationalization Path

Describe the smallest evidence program capable of distinguishing the candidate
from alternatives. Derive it from the predictions, not desired features.

## Generalization Boundary

State task, model/provider, environment, evidence, action, time, cost, and
population boundaries. Mark `Unknown`, `Unclear`, `Not established`, `Not
evaluated`, or `Conflicting evidence` where appropriate.

## Novelty and Contribution Claim

Distinguish the possible IRIS-X research contribution from commodity runtime,
evaluation, tracing, workflow, provider, or storage substrate. Treat novelty as
a claim requiring external-primary-source checking during the Ultra session.

## Unresolved Assumptions

List assumptions whose failure changes the candidate rather than merely its
implementation.

## Strongest Alternative Explanation

Steelman the strongest competing formulation and identify the evidence that
would make it preferable.

## Adversarial Disposition

Record: retain, revise, split, combine without erasing distinctions, reject, or
undecided. Give an evidence-grounded reason. This field is not a score and must
remain empty until all candidates receive symmetric adversarial review.
